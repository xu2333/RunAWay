# Short cards recovered the exact television and the chosen bundle, but lost optional offers

In a previously unmeasured real shopping task the owner asked for advance preparation and a timed buying guide. The current message named none of the products. The prior conversation supplied the eligible television series and screen size, a chosen accessory bundle and its seller, and an unavailable membership. Both full-history plans used the specific series and size when shopping. Both current-only plans produced a generic holiday checklist.

| Use exact brand, series and size for television eligibility | 0/2 | 1/2 | 0/4 | 4/4 |

| All six in the same plan | 0/2 | 1/2 | 0/4 | 0/4 |

**History signal and scoring sensitivity.** One full-history plan used the exact series and size at checkout while omitting the manufacturer's name throughout. The blinded analyst marked it partial under the literal brand/series/size requirement. The preregistered two-of-two strict history signal consequently did not pass. Accepting the manufacturer-specific series and size as sufficient would give history 2/2 for both the primary and joint scores. This sensitivity changes no other group. Both full-history plans preserved the confirmed purchase route, conditional discounts and rebate steps. The current-only plans lacked even the basic shopping list, so the difference includes ordinary task context.

**Where the two extractors diverged.** Both candidate cards (698 and 670 characters) bound the brand, exact series and size to offer eligibility during extraction, and both reads of each card used them to filter offers. The original cards (615 and 589 characters) already generalized the television in the detailed decision. Their deliveries advised the shopper to check the model without supplying it. All four cards retained the historical bundle price and stand. Only the candidate cards retained the chosen retailer and the named club restriction together, sufficient to give an identifiable nonmember route. The first original extraction still named the club in its detailed conditions, then omitted it from the card. The second lost the club's identity in the detailed decision. All four original reads received partial bundle and membership credit.

**Which savings survived.** Both reads of each candidate card distinguished the seller's marketplace category from a headline rebate and kept the payout separate from the amount paid. One original card also supported this in both reads; the other kept generic rebate and tracking advice but lost the seller-category condition. Separately, neither extractor carried a specific conditional television protection benefit or tablet payment-card discount into its detailed decisions or portable cards. Both full-history plans offered a concrete eligible branch and ordinary fallback. All eight card reads had only partial credit here. Correctly retaining one unavailable membership did not preserve the other, potentially applicable routes.

No delivery claimed new verified stock, current prices or an official start time without support. This is a historical, no-tools planning replay with one owner, two independent cards per method and two reads per card; it measured neither live deals nor human acceptance. The candidate fulfilled the repeated television-retention criterion on these cards. Neither short-card method fulfilled the complete-repair criterion. The comparison does not estimate cross-user accuracy.
