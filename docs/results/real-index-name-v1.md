# Restoring the index recovered the final population on three of four reads

The benchmark owner wanted leading models on a particular index evaluated after freezing the benchmark. Two archived short cards retained the procedure but generalized the index name. We inserted its name into the existing population phrase, adding ten characters to each card. Removing the insertion reproduced the original bytes. Each of the four views completed the same real planning task twice with the same reader. Current messages, reader instructions, and output schema were identical across conditions.

| Fixed view | Intended leaders | Non-target calibration | Freeze before final | Related-model independence | All four in a usable plan |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original, 664 characters | 0/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 0/2 full | 0/2 |
| Name restored, 674 characters | 1/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 0/2 full | 0/2 |
| Original, 631 characters | 0/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 0/2 full | 0/2 |
| Name restored, 641 characters | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 full | 2/2 |

The cells count full credit. All six independence judgments below full credit were partial. On population, the 664-character original had one partial and one absent, the 631-character original had two partials, and the 674-character named card had one fully correct and one absent. Every delivery gave an actionable plan.

**641-character view.** Both plans reserved the index leaders for final evaluation. Both allowed an available checkpoint from a target family to test plumbing, assigned item calibration to other models, and specifically excluded the related smoke run or size comparisons from independent validation. Its two original reads had described leaders on an unspecified overall ranking and stopped short of an operational overlap rule. The insertion added no instructions about overlap.

**674-character view.** One plan explicitly assigned the intended leaders to a later stage. The other stopped at local candidates and their scale comparisons even with the index name visible. Both kept development models separate and explained why rarity alone cannot establish independence. Neither defined how to treat related checkpoints as blind evidence. Writing down the name fixed the missing reference on the card; the reader still omitted it once in delivery.

**Other tradeoffs.** All four original plans expressly ruled out tuning questions to fit an expected ranking. Only one of the four named-view plans did so: one of two for the 674-character view and zero of two for the 641-character view. The other three received partial credit, for treating rankings as secondary or discouraging adaptation to target results generally. The underlying no-tuning guidance remained on both cards. Both views of both parents respected resource priority and qualified model availability. That priority was present in the current exchange.

The preregistered repeated-recovery criterion held for one of the two parents. These within-task observations cannot establish that adding the name caused the change in independence or the loss of explicit ranking safeguards. We tested one owner, one previously examined task, two selected cards and one reader. There was no neutral same-length insertion. This was a manual card intervention; automatic retention of names during extraction remains untested. Model choices were conditional plans. No current ranking lookup, GPU test or user acceptance was performed.

All eight requested gpt-6.1-sol calls completed with no replacements or missing deliveries after the protocol passed CI. Reported usage was 54,596 input and 5,299 output tokens, with zero cached input tokens. Monetary cost is unknown. The local analyst locked excerpt-backed judgments before revealing condition assignments; a model naming the index in its response could partially reveal the condition.
