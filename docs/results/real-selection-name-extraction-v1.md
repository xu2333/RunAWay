## Where the ranking safeguard went

The owner proposed comparing the observed order with expectations. The earlier assistant recommended treating that expectation as a prior hypothesis and prohibiting item changes to produce the desired order. The owner agreed to proceed with the approach. All four detailed extractions and all four portable cards explicitly preserved that prohibition.

Both reads of Original 1 applied it; neither read of Original 2 did. Neither read of Candidate 1 applied it. One read of Candidate 2 explicitly forbade retuning to a desired order. The other rejected making saturated questions harder merely to separate the ranks. That narrower rule earned partial credit: it leaves open changing items, weights or difficulty to obtain a preferred ordering elsewhere. Thus the candidate had one full, one partial and two absent safeguards versus two full and two absent for the original. The omission occurred in delivery. Variation across generated cards and repeated reads leaves the cause of the difference unresolved.

## A separate loss in calibration

One Candidate 1 plan used a small target-family checkpoint to calibrate item difficulty, then assessed a larger relative after freezing. It excluded the exact pilot as independent evidence and proposed another family for comparison, but left the pilot's relatives eligible without addressing their overlap. That delivery earned partial credit on both calibration and independence. Its sibling delivery confined the related checkpoint to pipeline checks. The card itself retained training-source checks and separation of development from final evaluation. Three other cards compressed detailed provenance checks to broader cross-family language; several of their plans nevertheless supplied an operational safeguard. Source attribution, scope and availability remained qualified. Seven of eight plans explicitly respected shared-resource priority, which the current exchange already disclosed.
