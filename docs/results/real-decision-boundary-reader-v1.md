**661-character card.** Both prior reads addressed the interpretation of rankings or expected results without explicitly excluding changes to the benchmark to achieve an expected order. One revised read did rule out repeatedly tuning the questions for that purpose during calibration. The same plan proposed using a small checkpoint for substantive calibration if it was outside the final list; it added another family, but did not resolve whether relatives of the pilot could provide independent evidence. Both revised reads placed method development before final testing but left the commitment to freeze the pool or scoring implicit. The other revised read treated a rank change as insufficient evidence of overfitting, which leaves open what to do with the evaluation if the order is surprising.

**662-character card.** Both readers applied the concrete ranking safeguard on both reads. One revised plan excluded size comparisons within a family as independent evidence and retained all five requirements. Its sibling used two related pilot checkpoints to inspect difficulty before adding a cross-family check. It still froze the evaluation and refused to alter the items to match an expected order, but its calibration and independence decisions were only partial. The prior reader also had one strict pass. The fixed card already supported two full ranking decisions with the prior reader in this batch.

This is a within-task diagnostic on two selected cards from one owner. The concurrent controls are fresh: the one-of-four ranking score from the preceding extraction study is historical. The fixed inputs locate the observed variation at delivery, but two reads per cell cannot establish that the instruction reliably causes either the gain or the losses. The result does not support replacing the existing reader.
