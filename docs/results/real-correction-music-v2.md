## What history changed

In R5, current-task-only and generic advice proposed escalating the conflict.
The history condition instead objected that the conflict undermined a previously
requested character quality. That is a qualitative signal of contextual use.
However, the actual short correction rejects the scene's old-status flavor,
while the prediction emphasizes the character being diminished. Those readings
are related but not unambiguously equivalent under the frozen criterion. We
preserve an unresolved judgment instead of adding a requirement from the later
assistant explanation or declaring a hit. Any later owner adjudication must be
recorded separately; these original scores and outputs stay unchanged.

In R3, history reinforced an earlier request for modern-life resonance, while
the next actual correction rejected excessive modernization. This may reflect a
newly clarified boundary between two goals. It does not prove a compression loss:
this condition received uncompressed available history.

In R2, all three responses prioritized the current explicit analysis request and
missed the earlier named inspiration that the owner next revisited. Its presence
in history did not make it the predicted next priority. In R4, history retrieved
a prior concern about wordiness and flow but missed the actual correction about
fragmentation and dramatic conflict. The two non-history conditions abstained.

These failures separate remembering an earlier statement from selecting which
one matters now. They also expose a limitation of predicting the single next
correction: a response may raise a reasonable alternative concern and still miss
the observed target. This study does not establish usefulness of the generated
prompt in actual future tasks, or absence of such usefulness.
