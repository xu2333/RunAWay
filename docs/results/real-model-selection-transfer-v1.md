# A short card kept the evaluation order and lost the target index

A real benchmark-design task asked which models to test if shared compute became available. The immediate request gave a model-family lead and operating constraints. Earlier, the owner had identified the leaders of a particular index as the final population and asked to use less prominent models in development. The owner accepted the direction after the assistant proposed freezing the evaluation before testing the targets.

Two cards were generated independently from the 44 earlier messages. Each reader received the same five current messages and the same fixed card twice. One analyst scored the twelve shuffled plans before opening the assignments or either card. Each cell below counts full credit across two deliveries.

| Current exchange | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

| 664 characters, original | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 0 |

| 631 characters, revised | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 0 |

Both history plans recovered the intended leaders. One proposed using a related checkpoint for development and labeling the overlap at analysis time. It acknowledged the concern but left independent calibration and the timing of the freeze only partially specified. The protocol required two joint passes for a repeatable history signal. The fixed-card reader improvement observed on demonstration learning did not replicate here.

## Where the information went

- **664-character card.** The cited source identified the index. The detailed claim already generalized it to an existing overall ranking. The portable card retained the broad goal, and neither reader identified the index in delivery.
- **631-character card.** The detailed claim named the index and assigned its leaders to final evaluation. The portable prompt shortened that to leaders on a public overall ranking. Both revised deliveries recovered this broader population, compared with neither original delivery specifying overall-ranking leaders. Both original deliveries still referred to popular targets. Several indices fit the wider description, hence partial credit for population. Giving credit to any overall-ranking leaders would change these two revised deliveries; that broader question was not the registered endpoint.
- **Other decisions.** All eight card deliveries separated calibration and formal evaluation. All eight only partially addressed related-model independence. Both full-history deliveries explicitly dealt with family overlap. Across the fixed cards, the revised reader explicitly retained shared-resource priority in four of four plans versus one of four original reads. The immediate exchange already supplied that constraint. The rule against changing questions to obtain a preferred ranking was explicit in three of four revised and two of four original reads. The remaining plans varied in detail; these within-task counts cannot establish a general reader effect.
- **Attribution and scope.** The cards distinguished the owner's population and study goal from technical safeguards proposed by the assistant. All twelve deliveries treated local checkpoints as conditional candidates. No live inventory, ranking lookup, GPU execution or independent human acceptance was measured.

The clearest repair to test is whether a portable card preserves a source-specified name when that name determines eligibility. Both cards had room under the 768-character ceiling. A matched comparison could isolate the name while keeping the rest of each card fixed and checking for changes to calibration, timing and independence. This repair has not been tested.

All 14 approved attempts completed on gpt-6.1-sol. Reported usage was 119,416 input tokens including 4,224 cached, plus 14,258 output tokens. Monetary cost was unavailable.
